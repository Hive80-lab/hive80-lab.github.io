The First 30 Minutes

An incident just started. You have no security staff. Here is exactly what to do — numbered, in order. Print this.

Minute 0–5: Don't panic, don't guess

  1. Say it out loud. Message one accountable person: "Possible incident, investigating, next update at :10". No heroics, no silence.
  2. Start a timestamped log (one shared doc). Every action, every time. You will need it twice: during, and after.
  3. Do NOT power off affected machines unless data is being actively destroyed. Memory and live state are evidence.

Minute 5–15: Scope it

  1. One question: what is actually broken or exposed? Write it as one sentence. If you can't, you're still gathering.
  2. Check the boring things first: is it a vendor outage? A failed update? An expired certificate? Half of "breaches" are Tuesdays.
  3. Identify affected systems: who can reach the affected service? What data does it touch? Customer data changes everything — see severity below.

Minute 15–30: Contain and communicate

  1. Cheap containment wins: revoke suspicious sessions, rotate exposed credentials, block the malicious source at the firewall, take the affected service offline if revenue loss < breach risk.
  2. Severity call (keep it simple):
    • SEV1 — customer data exposed or revenue stopped → all hands, exec informed now
    • SEV2 — internal data or single system affected → respond today
    • SEV3 — suspicious but unconfirmed → monitor, revisit in 24h
  3. First stakeholder message (copy-paste): "At [time] we detected [issue]. Impact: [known impact]. We have [action taken]. Next update: [time]." No speculation. No blame.

⏱ The trap to avoid

The first 30 minutes decide whether the incident costs you a bad afternoon or a bad quarter. Teams without a plan waste them on deciding who should respond. Decide now, while nothing is burning — that's what the drills are for.
